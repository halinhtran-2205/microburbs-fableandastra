03

Old sales still matter.

Over half of last year's sales had sold before, often a decade ago. That old price is a strong clue once it's brought up to date.

So we keep sales back to 2010 and adjust every price to today.

04

The market turned in 2026.

Prices rose from 2016 to a February 2026 peak, dipped in May and June, then held steady.

So we price at the July and August level and trim 2% to avoid reading high after the turn.

05

No single clue is enough.

Priced with one clue alone, at best half the flats land within 10%. Each clue is right where the others are wrong.

So we let a model combine them, which lifts the hit rate past 70%.

How the model works

1Adjust to today

Flats that sold twice show how prices moved each quarter.

2Gather clues

Earlier sales of the flat, its building, its block and suburb.

3Add details

Bedrooms, parking, floor and location, gaps filled from the API.

4Combine

A tree model weighs every clue. Three runs averaged, 2% trim.

Training and testing

The model only sees sales before the split. We then check it against real sales after it.

Train: sales from 2010 to the split.

Test: later sales under $1.2m, no shops, as the judges do.

Choosing the time window

Within 10%, test from Sep 2025

  • Before 2010 hurts. No bedroom counts, and prices from a different market.
  • After 2018 only hurts more. Many flats last sold in the early 2010s.
  • Other settings barely matter. Under one point of difference.

Model settings
  • Price index: Bailey, Muth and Nourse repeat-sales regression by quarter, smoothed between quarters.
  • Look-back clues are averages of earlier indexed sales only. A sale never sees itself or anything later.
  • LightGBM, Huber loss on log price, 1,500 rounds, recent sales weighted more (six-year decay), three seeds averaged, values lowered by 2%.
What did not work, and what we would do next
  • Cheap flats under $500k and buildings with little history are the weak spot.
  • A quarterly index is slow to catch a turn. A monthly index would react faster.
  • Shops, offices and car spaces get rough values. They are not scored.
  • Next: the direction a flat faces, views blocked by nearby buildings, and nearest sales for buildings with no history.
